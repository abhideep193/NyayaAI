### Answer

Yes. A child accused of an offence can get bail in India. When a person who is apparently a child is detained by police or brought before the Juvenile Justice Board, the law says they shall generally be released on bail, whether the alleged offence is bailable or non-bailable. Bail may be granted with or without a surety—a person who gives an assurance for the child's appearance. The child may instead be placed under the supervision of a probation officer or in the care of a suitable person. [jj_act_2015:12]

Release is not automatic in every case. It can be denied if there are reasonable grounds to believe that release is likely to bring the child into association with a known criminal, expose the child to moral, physical or psychological danger, or defeat the ends of justice. The Board must record its reasons and the circumstances for denying bail. [jj_act_2015:12]

If the police do not release the child, the child must be kept in an observation home or a place of safety until they can be brought before the Board. If the Board does not release the child, it must order placement in an observation home or a place of safety during the inquiry. [jj_act_2015:12]

If the child cannot meet the bail conditions within seven days of the bail order, the child must be brought before the Board so that the conditions can be modified. [jj_act_2015:12]
